# Canvas Layer

The canvas is the projection the user touches: cards and connections rendered for direct manipulation at speed-of-thought. It is the most mature layer; its foundation work is tracked in `research/03-performance-architecture/` and gated by the perf suites. Governing decisions: ADR 0010, ADR 0011, ADR 0019, ADR 0020, ADR 0021, ADR 0023, ADR 0024, ADR 0030.

How everything on this layer *looks* — surfaces, type, color meanings, control anatomy, motion, copy — is governed by the approved design system (`docs/design/design-system/SYSTEM.md`; its §8 specs the canvas surfaces specifically). Rendering work obeys both this document's invariants and that rule book; neither overrides the other.

Current performance decision: the canonical [Excalidraw](../../research/03-performance-architecture/12-excalidraw-rendering-performance-study.md) and [tldraw](../../research/03-performance-architecture/13-tldraw-rendering-performance-study.md) studies informed ADR 0023's Passive/Live authority contract and ADR 0024's visibility-aware image lifecycle. The measured [Reading-band architecture selection](../../research/03-performance-architecture/19-reading-band-architecture-selection.md) chose buffered rich-DOM residency, the existing Board-wide safety representation, separate Live and gesture-proxy authority, and visibility-aware image renditions. #555 makes the rendition resolver the production CardAssets and ImageGallery display path; #554 makes bounded, buffered, direction-aware, hysteretic passive residency (`P`) and the Board-wide safety representation (`S`) production; #556 makes additive Live ownership (`L`) and exactly-once first-intent activation production; #557 keeps complete long Results present before activation and preserves the clicked caret and relative reading position when long Note, Ask AI, and Conversation editors reopen; #560 makes pending/adopted rendition claims and last-reference invalidation safe under the production budgets. #753 adds a 600,000-unit per-viewport text-weight ceiling to ordinary cold `P`: exact-visible Cards are considered first, weight-rejected Cards remain fully painted in `S` without starving later ordered candidates, and independently owned `L` still wins. Founder field evidence adds a viewport-local oversize pool (`O`): up to 10 exact-visible Cards whose individual weight exceeds 100,000 mount as native Passive rich-DOM Cards, outside the ordinary weight pool but inside the shared 540-Card ceiling. Additional exact-visible oversized Cards remain in the existing painted `S` representation. `O` never admits look-ahead, preload, or offscreen retention. This does not retune Detail Band thresholds or make text weight a band-demotion input.

## Selected renderer architecture (P/O/S/L, semantic entry, and long-Card continuity production)

The renderer separates the current scene's viewport-visible set (`V`) from ordinary preload residency (`P`), exact-visible oversize residency (`O`), independently pinned Live owners (`L`), the bounded 0–2 lightweight/invisible hot overlay (`G`), active editors (`E ⊆ L`), and the Board-wide lightweight safety representation (`S`). Production `P` is a deterministic, hysteretic ordered subsequence: exact-visible Cards lead, followed by bounded direction-aware look-ahead, preload, and retained Cards, within both the real 540-Card ceiling and a 600,000-unit cumulative text-weight ceiling. A candidate that exceeds the remaining weight stays in `S` without ending admission for later ordered candidates. `O` admits Cards above the separate 100,000-unit individual threshold in the same stable prior-visible-first order, but only from exact `V`; it is capped at 10 Cards and never spends the ordinary 600,000-unit pool. `P ∪ O` still shares the terminal 540-Card ceiling. `V` is therefore the first admission tier, not an unconditional subset of `P ∪ O`; `V − (P ∪ O)` remains fully painted in `S` instead of becoming blank or changing the Detail Band. In Reading, a text-bearing `S` Card paints the existing cached plain-text summary inside its body, bounded to 90 source characters and at most six visible lines per Card; the whole frame also shares the painter's existing viewport-area text-line cap. It uses the same saved footprint established by the Card's first settled native render, so residency changes and reloads cannot shrink the frame before activation or grow it afterward. This excerpt is visual only: it adds no Markdown parsing, DOM, links, selection, Find, or accessibility authority, and mounted native Cards paint no excerpt beneath themselves. Production `L` remains an additive owner union, but pointer ownership is deliberately narrow: hover is presentation/geometry-only for every Card and never creates `L`; an eligible gesture may retain an already-resident Card, while `O` and `S` pointer presses defer Live ownership until React Flow's shared 5px threshold resolves a stationary click. Pointer travel keeps only geometry/`G` authority and cannot mount an editor. Programmatic focus/selection, editing, internal scrolling, active media, and an accepted click may still pin `L`; releasing one owner cannot evict a Card held by another. Rich-DOM residency is `P ∪ O ∪ L`, with one DOM owner per Card, and `E` is always contained by `L`. `G` is never rich Card DOM or editor authority.

While editable Reading opacity is positive, ordinary mounted `P` keeps its native focus and direct-interaction paths, but hover remains a CSS/painter presentation effect and does not wake Board ownership state. Exact-visible `O` and painter-owned `S` are click-resolved: hover and pointer travel do not activate them, their press latches geometry-only drag behavior, and only a stationary release on the click side of the shared 5px threshold creates visual selection. That outline commits first. A one-paint barrier then swaps interaction/editor selection, releases the outgoing Card's retained owner, and begins the accepted incoming `S`/`O` activation; newer rapid selections cancel stale pending work. `S` publishes that already-resolved click intent before mounting so the newly native Markdown surface receives the original point without synthetic event replay. The existing Card-colour dot uses the design-system loading pulse while the `S`/`O` click prepares Live state. Once genuine readiness arrives, the marker remains only long enough to guarantee a 500 ms minimum from transition start and then clears on the next frame; editor focus and usability are never delayed. Reduced motion removes both the animation and this visual minimum. Ordinary `P` and already-Live Cards never show the pulse. The painter remains until native ownership is visible, so activation has no blank frame. Programmatic focus/selection may still activate the matching Card directly. At zero Reading opacity the owner state machine is disabled: `G` may remain a bounded manipulation proxy, but it cannot create rich DOM or `L`.

Mounted rich Cards expose named Card groups with their kind, label, and selection state. `G` is explicitly unfocusable and absent from the accessibility tree, so keyboard or programmatic focus can never land on an invisible manipulation proxy. A Card focus request selects and centres its graph-derived target, then one scoped DOM observer focuses the exact rich Card when React Flow publishes it; the request cancels if the user chooses another live control first. Focus capture contributes the matching `L` owner. If a focused Card is unexpectedly removed and the browser has no live replacement focus, recovery lands on the named Board region; it never steals focus from an already-connected control. The bounded graph-derived Board search surface is the single off-residency keyboard and assistive-technology entry: activating a stable result reuses this focus request without widening cold `P ∪ O` residency or rendering every Card at once, and adds only the requested Card's existing `L` focus owner; it never pretends an unmounted Card is a tab stop.

Long Results keep their complete Markdown root before and during Live activation; the auto-height growth cap bounds layout by scrolling the Card body rather than truncating content or replacing late links. Selection authorizes point activation of a long Markdown editor only on the click side of React Flow's shared click/drag threshold, then focuses the actual contenteditable at the recorded release without first constructing a competing root-end selection. Pointer travel of an `O`/`S` Card remains geometry-only and does not select, acquire Live ownership, show loading, or mount the editor. A focused editor survives dragging another Card and ends only on real blur, a tool/scope change, or a stationary click on another Card, while Note, Ask AI, and Conversation re-entry retain the Card's relative internal scroll.

Viewport pan/zoom and Card drag keep the complete native Card DOM visible for every resident rich Card kind. Position and stacking changes are excluded from each Card's semantic subscription, so React Flow moves its shallow node wrapper without rerendering the Card subtree. Zoom-dependent connector, resize, and connection chrome reads zoom only at the small leaf that paints that chrome; no changing custom property inherits from the Board through semantic Card descendants. Reading crossfade likewise writes standard, non-inherited opacity on the exact visual-node wrapper. Descendant-wide gesture cursor and selection selectors are avoided so gesture state does not rematch an enormous prose subtree. During a held native resize, React Flow's outer frame intentionally keeps its begin geometry; the selected border temporarily paints on the contained Card resize layer that already follows hot geometry, then returns to the reconciled outer wrapper. The browser can therefore composite the existing Card while native hit targets, focus, text selection, internal scrolling, media, browser Find, and accessibility remain continuously present. Eido adds no motion-only text hiding and does not use within-Card virtualization, `visibility`, `content-visibility`, or a DOM-to-canvas snapshot overlay.

Internal reading position has a separate 1,024-entry true-LRU keyed by `(Idea id, Card id)`. It restores the exact scroll offset when the same read surface remounts and retains a relative offset when an editable Card crosses passive/editor shapes; stale entries may be evicted normally. This state is UI continuity only, never graph or Profile truth. Packaged drag/resize progress is also a separate boundary: the renderer owns gesture frames, the released Card paints before the coalesced strict whole-Profile capture runs, and checkpoint flush still forces the latest exact state. `cardScrollRetention.browser.test.ts`, `CardGeometryIsolation.test.tsx`, `giantNoteSelection.browser.test.ts`, `giantEditableCardIntent.browser.test.ts`, and `longResultMotion.perf.ts` guard continuity, isolation, release-gated editing, and paired short/stress Result motion; native Card browser suites remain the interaction authority.

Board-global cost remains limited to saved graph records, spatial bounds/indexes, viewport-independent metrics, lightweight scene planning/paint, and aggregate budget accounting. Rich DOM, Markdown construction, editor work, style/layout/paint, and image work are resident/object-local costs driven by `P ∪ O ∪ L` and actual visual demand. Visible-card occupancy remains the Reading band safety boundary. Board-wide text weight remains observable; the same immutable per-Card projection supplies cold `P` and exact-visible `O` admission without rescanning text on navigation. Text length never demotes the whole viewport out of Reading.

Image demand is keyed by the authoritative saved Card asset/reference, purpose, and display size. Compressed bytes are storage accounting, not identity or decoded-memory evidence. A visible, preloading, or Live visual consumer may pin its rendition; `G` alone may not. Directional prewarm reuses the scene's exact look-ahead candidates and runs only after both Board and rendition motion settle; the browser-idle callback rechecks motion, and its single-flight coordinator delegates the unchanged entry and byte budgets to the resolver. Decoded source pixels, derived-rendition residency, paint/upload cost, and cache growth/eviction/revocation remain separate gates. #413's 8 MiB decoded and 2 MiB derived-rendition thresholds are provisional evidence values, not universal device promises.

Native browser toolbar Find remains limited to currently mounted DOM and is inherited from an unchanged complete visible Card; its toolbar UI is not independently automated, and `window.find` smoke is not a toolbar oracle. Eido's graph-derived Board search and logical whole-Board copy are the complete production equivalents for offscreen/unmounted content; neither widens cold `P ∪ O` residency or manufactures new `L` owners. Link activation, keyboard traversal, accessibility-tree completeness, and focus continuity remain mandatory production gates, not accepted losses. Versions, provenance, settings, and other cold data remain #408's secondary neutral lane and cannot change this residency or image authority.

Editor source reconciliation is also explicit. `MarkdownCardEditor` keeps local editor echoes in the mounted editor instead of importing the same giant value through the passive renderer. A true external Graph replacement increments a source revision before calling the editor's import seam, so an external rollback to bytes equal to an earlier source still replaces the live document; local echoes do not. This preserves the hot editor transaction while retaining an authoritative external replacement path.

## Invariants (do not break)

1. **The canvas renders the graph; it must not become the graph database** (CONTEXT.md). Graph truth lives in the store; canvas state is derived and rebuildable.
2. **No full-board work on interaction frames** — typing, dragging, streaming, pointer movement, and animation frames must not trigger full-board scans or index rebuilds (ADR 0010). `createBoardScene` no longer serialises board text or derives card summaries: viewport-independent `deriveBoardCardMetrics` updates only on graph changes, and navigation re-derives the scene with zero card-metric visits. Full rebuild fallbacks remain explicit and counted; do not add silent full-board work. The in-memory Graph and mounted editor are the hot interaction authority, so typing updates the live editor and Graph without awaiting Profile capture. Routine text, editor-completion narrative events, and released geometry schedule one shared 3,000 ms post-paint quiet window, where the existing renderer queue captures only the newest exact state. One native commit may be in flight and one newest projection may wait behind it. Ordinary non-gesture durable mutations, lifecycle operations, Agent barriers, and authoritative checkpoints retain their immediate durability boundary, and checkpoint flush forces deferred routine work to the latest exact Board first.
3. **User-perceived stability over raw FPS**: stable LOD (no detail flashing), 0 blank frames, 0 missing visible cards, readable-zoom full-detail aperture, working mouse/trackpad pan and wheel zoom. These are gated — keep the gates green.
4. **React Flow stays behind the Board.tsx boundary**; graph core stays canvas-agnostic. Eido is not migrating to the tldraw SDK (CONTEXT.md).
5. **The overview underlay under fully opaque mounted cards — a Reading-band concern: represented while moving, invisible when settled** (the #126→#158→#178→#215 tug-of-war). While the viewport moves, every visible card stays represented — the dense full-detail perf gate requires it. A mounted `P ∪ O ∪ L` Card's underlay draws as a *fill-only silhouette*: pixel-aligned to its measured DOM size, with the shared 8px Card-shell radius transformed by zoom, and with no normal/selected stroke, ports, or mid-detail content. The mounted DOM Card owns all boundary ink, so the underlay cannot leak a second outline while preserving fill continuity. The default translucent Group is the fill exception: while its motion silhouette owns the shared 45% resting or 55% selected wash, the DOM Group temporarily yields that wash so two translucent layers cannot stack into an opaque frame. In both DOM and canvas stacking, Groups occupy the plane below connections and ordinary Cards, so a colour wash cannot cover member wires or connector nodes. A painter-owned `S` Card remains a full Overview rendition with its existing radius and stroke grammar. Once settled, fully opaque `P ∪ O ∪ L` Cards are fully suppressed while `S` stays painted. The Reading↔Overview crossfade is distinct: mounted bodies use inverse opacity while nonmounted `S` bodies and ordinary labels remain fully opaque. `mountPlanForScene` is the downstream hot owner of this occlusion: visual `L` outside cold `P ∪ O` enters `paintOccludingCardIds` and settled `overviewSuppressedCardIds`; invisible `G` never does. Guards: `BoardOverviewLayer.test.ts` (silhouette boundary ownership and geometry), `overviewGhosting.browser.test.ts` (alignment + fill continuity + Group opacity continuity), `groupConnectorVisibility.browser.test.ts` (Group → connection → ordinary Card stacking), `cardEditing.browser.test.ts` (settled suppression), `lodUniformity.browser.test.ts` (crossfade opacity + interaction), and the dense full-detail gate in `browserInteraction.perf.ts` (representation). Suppression must never be re-widened outside that bounded crossfade to fix a visual artifact — that breaks representation; fix it at the draw seam instead. In `BoardOverviewLayer`'s card paint loop, adding the label candidate must remain the last statement: an early `continue` there silently drops the Reading silhouette candidate before the label layer can own its suppression.
6. **One authoritative representation per Card and band across DOM and canvas, with complementary crossfades.** Reading keeps one global fidelity band but partitions visual ownership: cold-admitted `P ∪ O` and hot `L` own rich DOM, while overflow `V − (P ∪ O)` owns the painted `S` rendition even at rest. Overview and Map mount no visual rich Cards and paint their respective canvas representation. Inside the Reading↔Overview window, mounted `P ∪ O ∪ L` visuals crossfade against inverse-opacity Overview bodies while nonmounted `S` stays fully opaque; at Reading opacity zero, rich DOM unmounts and canvas interaction takes authority for every Card. Card-dense viewports that exceed the visible-card ceiling stay canvas-only; text weight changes residency within Reading but never changes the band. Map↔Overview remains a canvas-only crossfade. The label layer is a screen-space overlay governed by priority and collision, so it is intentionally non-uniform. Reading-band candidates that are not mounted bypass both `labelBandEligible` and the tier check—deliberately broader than the landmark-plus-hover grant below Reading—because their DOM titles are absent. “Uniform across the screen at one instant” now describes the fidelity band, not a promise that every Reading Card has the same lifecycle owner. Guards: `lodUniformity.browser.test.ts`, `boardScene.test.ts`, and `zoomChrome.test.ts`.
6a. **The Title card is the one kind with no low-detail stand-in.** A Title card
   is type the founder drew at a size, so the painter reproduces the DOM label
   at board scale instead of substituting a card shape plus a fixed-size name:
   no paper fill, no ports, no border at rest, and nothing at all painted under
   a mounted one (a silhouette fill would put paper behind transparent type).
   It never enters the screen-space label layer, which would double-draw its
   name at a size the founder did not choose. Both halves size type through the
   same rule (`fitFontSize`), so a band crossing changes who draws and never
   what is drawn. The cost this accepts: a small Title card scales down with the
   board like everything else, so at Map it is only as readable as the founder
   drew it big. Guards: `BoardOverviewLayer.test.ts` (painted Title cards),
   `titleCardFontFit.test.ts`, `titleCardTypeFit.browser.test.ts`.

7. **The board scene is cold.** `createBoardScene` is a pure function of the board graph projection and viewport. Pointer state—hovered id, gesture participants, drag/connect preview—stays hot in the adapter and is composed *after* the scene by `mountPlanForScene`. A pointer move that does not change target identity must not re-derive the scene, rebuild React Flow node/edge arrays, write the store, invalidate the label cache, or acquire Live ownership. Below the Reading crossfade, zero visual Card bodies mount; the only React Flow mounts are 0–2 `gestureProxy` interaction nodes, and LOD Card-mount gates exclude them. These proxies are `G`, never `L`; independently accepted focus, interaction, media, scroll, or resident-gesture lifecycles own any matching Live Card. A proxy remains bodyless and its resize controls are transparent interaction targets. When a painted Card is selected, `BoardOverviewLayer` paints its four solid corner grabbers in the same pass and from the same frame as the Card, so a browser control can never visually outrun the Card. Selection—not proxy hover lifetime—owns those grabbers: expiry of the temporary proxy must not clear a canvas-painted selection; an explicit outside click does. During resize, the held interaction target follows the raw pointer directly, while painted geometry, visible grabbers, and saved size coalesce to at most one update per rendered frame; release flushes the exact final size without requiring reselection. The design-system resize readout and painted grabbers consume this shared hot geometry; they do not create a second resize owner or retune the production bounds. Guards: C2's pointer-storm gate, C3's two-proxy stability gate, `lowZoomResize.browser.test.ts`, and the source pin that `BoardSceneOptions` contains no pointer field (ADR 0020).
8. **Paint and hit-test share geometry and authoritative interactive membership.** The ownership partition is explicit: mounted `P ∪ O ∪ L` Cards receive pointer/keyboard events from their DOM; painter-owned `S` Cards are the scene's canvas `hitTestCandidates`. Complementary crossfade bodies and motion silhouettes may still paint beneath DOM owners, but they are not a second canvas hit target. `paintedPortGeometry`, `paintedBadgeRadius` / `paintedConnectionBadgeGeometry`, and `planPaintedConnections` respectively own port discs, badge discs, and painted connection membership for drawing and canvas hit testing; the shared badge-suppression predicate likewise owns badge membership. Eido deliberately does not materialise per-frame render-buffer arrays: sharing is through pure functions. Density caps are planned omissions with counters, never silent loop cutoffs. Port discs paint on every canvas representation, Map and Overview alike; concretely, direct canvas-port targeting remains `band !== 'reading'`, and Reading `S` relies on proximity promotion to the real `L` handles before contact. A no-prior-movement descendant press is the explicit residual recorded above. Connector nodes stay at a stable screen size through canvas LOD; rich DOM may grow them only in close-up Reading. A held connector may finish on any surface of a semantically connectable Card: native opposite-Handle completion stays authoritative, while other releases use the mounted node stack and, when that stack cannot resolve the surface or native snap reports a nearby incompatible Handle, one exact release-time query against the existing incremental spatial index. This keeps pointer-transparent surfaces such as Groups hittable without letting an incompatible port discard an adjacent body drop. The fallback never enters pointer-move or scene derivation, and graph connection rules still own endpoint admission and orientation. The rich-DOM edge adapter subscribes to each endpoint node and derives the connector centre from its reactive measured frame; it never depends on React Flow's transform-sensitive cached handle bounds. The visible wire and authored arrow tip therefore terminate at the same card-edge midpoint as the painter through zoom and representation handoff. A Reading connection whose remote Card leaves bounded DOM residency transfers to the already-bounded canvas query, while an O(1)-per-edge, commit-coalesced mount registry suppresses real DOM owners there so exactly one representation remains without scanning all edges on navigation frames. Once Reading has measured an auto-height DOM Card, that measurement remains its exact derived footprint after the Card unmounts in Overview/Map; card paint and culling, connection endpoints, body/port hit tests, and gesture proxies must all consume it without gating on current React mount membership. Guards: `BoardOverviewLayer.test.ts`, `pointerTargets.test.ts`, `connectionFeel.browser.test.ts`, `anyZoomConnect.browser.test.ts`, and `overviewGhosting.browser.test.ts`.
9. **Select owns marquee selection at every LOD.** Select turns an empty-pane primary-button drag into a marquee in Reading, Overview, and Map; Move owns primary-button camera pan. The marquee unions mounted and painter-owned geometry, publishes live painted selection before release, and never fits the viewport to its result. Modifier-click adds painter-owned Cards to the current selection, a low-LOD gesture proxy moves the complete selected Card set by one shared delta, and edge auto-pan queries the spatial index across the full held flow-space rectangle so Cards beyond the original view join the same gesture without losing earlier hits. A marquee result is exactly a Shift-click multi selection: only the Cards themselves stay interactive, the blank canvas between them keeps the pane's own hover, press, and box-start behaviour, and dragging any selected Card moves the whole set. React Flow's own selection rectangle (`nodesSelectionActive`) is therefore never mounted. Dismissal is one-way: the gesture channel keeps publishing the committed result until the Card node data catches up, so a dismissed selection cannot repaint for a frame. Guard: `marqueeSelection.browser.test.ts`.
10. **Selecting an editable Markdown card must not reflow it.** Note, Ask AI, and Conversation bodies keep the same font metrics, block spacing, line breaks, table grid, link geometry, and card bounds when the passive projection is replaced by the editor. A single user-authored prose newline stays a visible line break through save, passive rendering, and editor re-entry; a writer's blank lines are content, so one blank line is the paragraph boundary and every empty paragraph beyond it keeps its own line box on both surfaces, including at the head and foot of a Note where the editor library would otherwise trim it. Creating a new line while editing always scrolls the caret line into view, whether or not it has any characters yet. Safe Markdown links render and activate while passive; direct URLs pasted over selected editor prose wrap that prose, and selecting an editor link opens a compact out-of-flow preview/edit/remove surface. Rich editor controls stay out of document flow, and the heavy editor mounts only for the selected card. Guards: `cardEditing.browser.test.ts` compares card-relative text runs and complete bounds across paragraphs, headings, inline styles, quotes, lists, rules, fenced code, tables, links, and marks for all three card kinds; `markdownWhitespace.browser.test.ts` covers the newline round trip; `markdownLinks.browser.test.ts` covers safe path rendering, passive activation, URL-over-selection, link editing, and exact passive/editor link geometry; `noteEditRenderParity.browser.test.ts` measures exact passive/editor line position and Card scroll across the running app; `noteBlankLinesWithPanels.browser.test.ts` covers blank-line survival together with the attachment panels sharing the same Card.
11. **A library template is never a board dependency.** The canvas can stamp a template, but the resulting Mod is a frozen deep copy owned by the Board with fresh stable nested IDs. Editing or removing a Profile-owned library template cannot change a stamp already on a Board (ADR 0030).
12. **A pasted website remains an ordinary Note, not an embedded browser or a separate Card kind.** One whole explicit HTTP(S) URL or scheme-less registrable domain creates an atomic Note whose editable body retains the pasted label and whose normalized source URL drives one optional inset preview projection. Scheme-less recognition uses the platform URL parser plus a packaged ICANN Public Suffix List lookup, not DNS, fetch, or a popularity list; explicit HTTP(S) remains the escape hatch for reserved or newly delegated suffixes. The renderer rejects non-host-shaped text locally and delegates only plausible scheme-less candidates across the installed bridge or the local development route, which keeps suffix data out of the canvas bundle. Recognition has a short deadline and falls back to an ordinary Note if the local boundary stalls; changing Ideas while recognition is pending cancels the paste rather than writing onto the wrong board. Preview loading never executes remote page code in the renderer, never creates an iframe/webview, and never replaces the Note body. Remote work is bounded to eight active plus sixteen waiting loads, and media crosses the boundary only as a dimension-bounded still PNG/JPEG. Failure settles to an actionable website-shaped fallback and cannot downgrade previously saved rich metadata. A reopened ready preview renders its durable metadata without background network work; Refresh/Retry is explicit and retains keyboard focus. Editing prose preserves the preview, while explicit Remove preview drops only that projection as one undoable action and leaves the current Note text unchanged. Prose, multiple links, editor-local pastes, bare or unknown suffixes, common dotted filenames, and supported asset payloads keep their existing clipboard routes. Paste is not the only trigger: a Note's own debounced body text can grow the same preview from its first `http(s)` URL, skipping fenced/inline code, as long as the Note carries no preview yet; each URL spends at most one attempt per Note, remembered in the Card's `meta` bag so it is never retried, and Remove preview remembers the removed URL as dismissed so the body watcher cannot immediately regrow it. Guards: `src/shared/linkPreview.test.ts`, `electron/linkPreviewUrlClassifier.test.ts`, `clipboardTextPaste.browser.test.ts`, `NotePreviewComposition.test.tsx`, `NoteLinkPreview.test.tsx`, `linkPreview.store.test.ts`, and the Electron preview contract/service tests.
13. **A local HTML preview becomes one private static asset inside an ordinary Note.** A whole absolute Finder/File Explorer `.html` or `.htm` path is only a renderer-side shape candidate. Electron main canonicalizes a bounded regular file, retains its physical path in a separate device-local SQLite source registry, and returns only an opaque source ID plus basename to the Board. If recognition fails or misses its short deadline, the fallback Note stores only that basename—never the physical path. That UI-sensitive registry uses a fail-fast SQLite connection with `busy_timeout=0`; it never inherits the Profile runtime's one-second wait. Capture uses a short-lived, sandboxed, JavaScript-disabled, network-blocked offscreen window behind a two-active/sixteen-waiting gate and deadline; local subresources are asynchronously realpath-confined to the source directory tree, and the window is destroyed after one bounded 16:9 JPEG. One shared image contract admits the 1200×675 logical capture and bounded high-DPI variants up to 2400×1600, with at most 700,000 bytes, exact decoded-size agreement, and JPEG boundary markers. Settlement writes that JPEG through the ordinary immutable Card-asset/Profile-blob path, while the Note stores only opaque source metadata and the asset ID. The preview JPEG is filtered out of the Note's ordinary image gallery so it renders exactly once inside the preview element; other image/file attachments remain independent. Ready Notes and reopened Boards render the saved image through the existing bounded rendition resolver and never recreate an iframe, webview, or HTML renderer; Refresh is the explicit recapture path, superseded source fingerprints cannot settle after newer work, and the previous static image remains visible while work is pending or the original becomes unavailable. Remove preview preserves the Note body and unrelated assets while removing the preview projection and its private JPEG. Guards: `localHtmlPageCapturer.test.ts`, `localHtmlPreviewService.test.ts`, `localHtmlPreviewSourceStore.test.ts`, `NoteLocalHtmlPreview.test.tsx`, `NotePreviewComposition.test.tsx`, `linkPreview.store.test.ts`, and the SQLite GUI projection reopen proof.
14. **An attachment carousel mounts only the tiles it can show.** A Note's image and preview panels (`AttachmentPanel.tsx`) each keep one box per attachment so scroll width, tile widths, and panel position stay exact whether or not a tile has mounted anything inside it; a tile mounts its real content only while it sits within one panel width of what the panel currently shows, watched by a single `IntersectionObserver` rooted on the track. A layout-time seed of the same box test runs before first paint, because the observer's first delivery arrives a task after the frame that mounted the track. This keeps a Card with a hundred attachments from paying to mount a hundred of them the moment it enters the Reading band; Overview and Map still mount no attachment DOM at all. Guard: `noteAttachmentPanels.browser.test.ts`.
15. **A panel that arrives pays for its own room, once.** An attachment panel is pinned to the bottom of the Card, so a link preview or a first image that appears while the founder is looking at a Note would otherwise take its height straight out of the Note body. The Card grows by exactly one panel floor (`--e-attachment-panel-height-min`) for each PANEL that arrives, never per tile: a second link preview joins the existing track and buys nothing. The decision is a marker in the Card's settings envelope (`attachmentPanelGrowth.ts`, ADR 0040), not something derived from the height, because a height cannot say whether it was already granted; that marker is what stops a reload, an undo, or a re-render from growing the same Card again. Panels a Note mounts with are recorded as paid without a resize, so opening a Board never rearranges it, and an auto-height Card is only ever marked, because it already measures the panel itself and writing a height would freeze it. Growth has no inverse today: removing the last preview does not shrink the Card back, because that height may since have been set by hand. Guards: `attachmentPanelGrowth.test.ts`, `noteAttachmentPanels.browser.test.ts`.
16. **An attachment carousel rests where the track actually ends.** Snap positions account for the track's own gutter (`scroll-padding-inline`), so the left end settles at `scrollLeft: 0` with the first tile whole rather than one padding in with it clipped, and the right end keeps the last tile's trailing gutter. Guard: `noteAttachmentPanels.browser.test.ts`.
17. **Wheel ownership is classified by the DOMINANT axis of a gesture.** A trackpad flick carries jitter on the other axis that comes and goes event by event, so any rule that reads "did the vertical delta move at all" relabels one sideways flick several times and breaks the gesture-continuity window that keeps momentum inside a Card. The horizontal end of a carousel therefore has to settle exactly like the vertical end of a Note body: the tail of the gesture that reached the edge stays in the Card, and only a NEW gesture pans the board. Guard: `cardInteraction.test.ts`.

The selected residency policy

DECIDED Ordinary cold Cards share a 600,000-unit pool in stable visible-first, prior-visible-first order. A Card that would exceed the remaining pool stays in S, but the planner continues to later candidates. That removes head-of-line starvation without reordering Cards or solving an expensive packing problem.

DECIDED A Card whose individual weight is above 600,000 cannot fit that pool by definition. If it is exact-visible, it may instead enter O: a native, passive/read-only-at-rest Card surface that preserves real Markdown, links, selection, and accessibility. O has 10 slots, consumes none of the 600K ordinary pool, shares the overall 540-Card ceiling, and never admits look-ahead, preload, or offscreen-retained Cards. Interaction promotes any Card into limit-exempt L.

The briefly considered 100-Card cap was rejected. Because every O Card is above 600K, 100 slots would permit more than 60 million weight units before accounting for ordinary or Live Cards. Ten is the evidence-led rung; changing it requires an actual 10-giant-Card viewport result.

What the “character budget” really counts

The label is shorthand. The immutable per-Card residency weight is:

Card block text length + chosen Result version text length + (asset count × 20,000)

  • Result history is handled correctly for the reported concern. The metric resolves chosenVersionId (falling back to the latest version) and counts only that version's main text.length. Nine unchosen versions do not accumulate against the viewport pool.
  • Conversation/follow-up depth is not fully modeled. Chosen transcript and follow-up-thread text are not added to this proxy today. That can undercount an unusually large visible Result surface and remains a named tuning follow-up.
  • Every asset contributes a fixed 20,000 units. This includes each pasted image and file. It is not the data URL length, saved byte size, decoded byte size, or a claim that an image equals 20,000 characters. The separate rendition cache still enforces its 8 MiB decoded and 2 MiB derived-byte budgets.

DECIDED Keep the cheap proxy for this repair. It is viewport-independent, incrementally cached, and good enough to prevent obvious rich-DOM blowups. Retune its components only with a Card type that contradicts the proxy in founder testing.

Technical teaching: five letters, one Card owner

One-minute model: V says what the viewport can see. P, O, and L are three reasons a real native Card is mounted. S is the lightweight canvas safety representation for visible Cards that have no native owner. The native union is exactly P ∪ O ∪ L; interaction and paint must consult that union, never assume P alone covers the screen.

How one visible Card gets an owner

Mechanism → trade-off → product meaning → gate

  • Residency: immutable Card metrics feed one deterministic planner. Skipping a miss avoids starvation; O10 restores native content for individually giant visible Cards. The trade-off is a bounded worst case of 10 giant native surfaces. The gate is a real 10-giant viewport, not a speculative cap increase.
  • Marquee: React Flow supplies selected mounted ids while Eido computes the same rectangle against S Card geometry, then unions both sets. The trade-off is one app-side geometry pass per coalesced selection frame. The gate is the mixed Reading interaction test plus founder feel.
  • Motion underlay: canvas draws the transformed Card fill with the same 8px shell radius; it draws no stroke, ports, or selection ring for native owners. The trade-off is intentionally less painter decoration under the DOM. The product result is a single boundary; the gate is pixel inspection while moving and settling.

What else can be tuned—and what should not move without evidence

  • 600K ordinary weight: founder-calibrated and merged. It is not the only bound.
  • 10 oversize exact-visible Cards: the new independent bound. It is the next manual stress surface.
  • 540 native cold Cards: an existing occupancy/count ceiling shared by P ∪ O; exceeding the visible count still leaves the scene canvas-owned rather than mounting unbounded DOM.
  • Live owners: L is deliberately limit-exempt while real interaction holds it. A pathological many-selected or many-media scenario is a separate ownership question, not a reason to make active work disappear.
  • 20K per asset: provisional residency weighting, independent from 8 MiB decoded and 2 MiB derived rendition-cache budgets.
  • Result content: only the chosen version's main text counts; unchosen history does not bottleneck residency, but chosen transcript/follow-up depth can be underweighted.
  • Painter fallback: an 11th individually oversize visible Card remains S; it is spatially present and interactive but not a full native Markdown/accessibility surface until L or capacity admits it.

Retro: what worked, what cost time, what changes next

One-minute verdict: The counters paid for themselves by turning “blank until hover” into an exact admission bug, and founder screenshots exposed two related ownership regressions quickly. The avoidable part was shipping a stable-prefix policy and “fully detailed only” marquee assumption without first enumerating every mixed P/O/L/S consumer. The corrective rule is simple: whenever ownership partitions change, audit paint, hit, selection, accessibility, diagnostics, and motion as one boundary.

Friction and the real unblocks

The original 300K policy was internally coherent but product-wrong: its tests intentionally pinned “stop at the first miss.” Raising the number alone could not solve a 4.86M head Card. A deterministic disposable repro falsified stale-cache theories: shrinking the blocker immediately admitted all later Cards. That led to the merged skip-and-continue policy instead of a larger arbitrary number.

The field repair then revealed a broader systems issue. Once Reading could legitimately mix native and painted Cards, marquee availability still assumed every visible Card must be native, and the moving painter still assumed it owned the Card border. Both bugs were old single-plane assumptions exposed by the new partition, not evidence that bounded residency itself should be removed.

Founder interventions

The founder's “600K feels fine” decision prevented calibration churn. The request for frozen native giant Cards changed the solution from an unbounded budget increase to a separate viewport-local pool. The 100-versus-10 exchange was especially useful: it made the worst-case cost visible and ended at the safer 10-Card rung. Screenshots of the sharper outline and the marquee disappearance then supplied concrete acceptance targets.

The cost was late-cycle scope movement across three related symptoms. That was justified by real field evidence, but future epics should schedule one explicit counter-guided founder pass after the first packaged candidate and before closeout language is written. One bundled pass would preserve the same product input with less tracker and documentation churn.

Agent accountability

The team should have challenged the “stable prefix” comment as a product choice, not merely an implementation invariant, and should have included giant-then-tiny Cards in the first residency boundary test. It also should have traced every consumer of “fully detailed” before accepting mixed Reading ownership. The founder did not create these bugs by testing late; the implementation and review lanes failed to anticipate the ownership fan-out.

The recovery was disciplined: no real Profile data was inspected, the symptom was reproduced in a disposable environment, visual/performance automation was not inflated, and the branch stayed one reversible repair. The remaining responsibility is to keep the pre-PR/merged boundary honest until hosted and packaged proof exists.

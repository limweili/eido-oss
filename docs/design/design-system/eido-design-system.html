The brief

BRIEF.md

What we are making, and the handful of rules we won't break. Read this first. Everything after it is this brief, made specific.

THE FEEL · 1 OF 2

Take away one thing: Eido is an instrument, not a document. Next on this route is colour, which is where the instrument gets its temperature.

Eido looks like a flat hardware instrument laid over warm paper, a Teenage Engineering control surface printed on a Nothing-EQ-calm grey-on-grey field, where your thoughts are the printed matter and the chrome is the machine.

Every decision on this page traces back to that sentence. It also does the refusing for us: paper has no glass, no glow and no gradient shading; instruments have no fake texture; print carries nothing that isn't information.

Two materials

one or the other, never both
Paperthe thought

Everything you read or write is Open Sans, sentence case, ink on paper. Notes, results, transcripts. Paper is still: it never moves on its own.

Instrumentthe machine

Everything the machine puts there is engraved in JetBrains Mono: buttons, values, labels, tools. The instrument moves with weight: it lifts, displaces, settles.

How to tell them apart

If you could have typed it, it is paper. If the machine put it there, it is instrument. A note body is paper. The word RUN on a button is engraved into the machine. A result's markdown is paper; the version chips above it aren't.

What we are protecting

someone in the middle of thinking
The canvas is quiet

Chrome whispers. The colour on screen belongs to your thoughts, not to our buttons.

Five steps ahead

Select a card and its actions appear. Finish a connection and the pulse travels toward what it feeds. A result lands with copy, note and follow-up already at the foot. Nobody should hunt for the obvious next move.

Consequence is visible

Anything that changes what AI will do shows what it changed, right where you changed it. The instrument never hides its wiring.

No instructions

You find things by hovering, by the cursor changing, by things always being in the same place. Copy explains what will happen, never how to operate the app.

Eight rules we design by

1 · Identity top-left, actions top-right, value at the foot. On every card, panel and control. Identity stays put, actions appear on hover, state reads at the bottom.

2 · A control shows its own value. A line's thickness is its strength. A slider's fill is its position. A row of version chips is the history. Numbers confirm; drawings tell you.

3 · One accent, one job. Green is selection, primary action and helps. Warm red is avoid and destructive. Purple is Mod, blue is conversation, yellow means needs your eye. A colour never moonlights.

4 · Depth comes from value, not shadow. The warm-grey ladder does the separating. The one shadow we own is worn only while something is lifted or floating.

5 · State changes colour, not geometry. Selecting, focusing, hovering and editing recolour and re-border. They never resize, reflow or shift anything.

6 · Refuse by staying quiet. Invalid targets don't light up. Actions you can't take don't appear. No error flashes for things you were never allowed to do.

7 · Open up one ring at a time. Rest, hover, selection, editing. Popover before panel before modal, and a modal only when the decision genuinely has to block.

8 · Escalate honestly. A run that fails says so where it failed, plainly, with the next step. No toast for something that happened right in front of you.

What we never do

the anti-slop list

No glass. No blur, no frosted panels, no translucency.

No resting shadows, no glows. Ever.

No gradients as shading. Flat colour only.

No pure black or white, no cool greys. One warm family.

No emoji, no glyph icons. Lucide strokes only.

No instructional copy. No "click here to", no "drag to".

No AI-cliché words. Elevate, seamless, unleash, powerful, magic.

No Title Case. Sentence case, and caps only for engraved micro-labels.

No spinners, no shimmer. Say "Running", show the one pulse, or lay out skeleton paper.

No colour on its own. Always pair it with a symbol, a line character or a word.

Nothing over 400ms. A replay you asked to watch is the one exception.

No new fonts, radii, shadows or hues. If it isn't a token, it doesn't exist.

What this is not

Not an inverted light theme

Night has its own designed values. Your colours stay your colours; the palette changes what things mean, it doesn't run a filter over the app.

Not a behaviour grab bag

Every interaction the app already has stays exactly as it is, unless a change is approved in PROPOSALS.md.

Not the Mod prototype grid

The prototype's fixed grid doesn't come with us. The board keeps free placement. What carries over is the tokens, the two voices, the surface ladder, the motion budget and where things sit.

The rules

The colour rulesAsk for a meaning. The system hands you the colour.

At night

designed, not inverted
Dark modeThe instrument at night. Designed, not inverted. Toggle it in the nav.

Warm graphite, never blue-black. The night is anchored on the card, #2B2725, with ground at #1E1B19 and panel at #25221F. Every shade keeps the rung it holds by day, so the ladder runs darkest to lightest in the same order in both modes. The steps between rungs are retuned for the dark, because equal steps on paper are not equal steps in the dark. One warm grey family throughout, not a warm one by day and a neutral one at night.

Ink becomes paper. Text flips to warm off-white, and ink-solid chrome (tooltips, pressed keys, active menu rows) flips to paper labels that read like lit signs.

Solids brighten one step and wear night text. Go, avoid, mod, convo lift toward the lamp; the words cut into them are near-black night, not white. Amber already glows and keeps its ink.

Light replaces shadow. Elevation is said by the surface getting lighter, never by something falling on it. The hairline is not heavier at night: it carries a lower alpha (.44 against daylight's .52) and lands on the same measured 3.2 to 3.7:1, because ink and paper have swapped places and equal alpha would not have meant an equal edge. The one lift shadow deepens, but structure never depends on it. Every hex on this page re-reads live when you toggle.

03

Grain

texture is material, not lighting

The one textured surface we allow, and the few moments it's for. Flat brand colour with a printed grain over it, like riso ink on paper. Never gloss, never a light source. Keep it for onboarding, empty boards and milestones: the moment it turns up in working chrome, it stops being special.

The recipefour flat layers

1 · One flat brand field. Green, blue, amber, or paper. Never a user colour, never a functional hue.

2 · At most two soft bleeds. Radial washes of the neighbouring brand hues, large and off-centre, like ink soaking into the sheet. The brief bans gradients as shading, because shading implies a light source and a light source implies depth. A bleed implies wet ink on a flat sheet. Nothing it touches gains a third dimension.

3 · The grain. One monochrome fractal noise, printed into the field: the same tile everywhere, and it never animates. On a brand field it prints both ways; on paper it can only darken, the way ink does.

4 · One flat thing on top. A glyph or a line of type, always above the grain, never textured itself.

Where it's allowed: onboarding, empty states, Idea covers, milestone moments, brand cards. Never on buttons, cards, fields, or anything you operate, and at most one textured surface visible at a time.

Of the two fields beside this, only the green one is painted in the product today, on the Idea cover. Paper is the sanctioned recipe for an empty state and nothing has needed it yet. Both are here because the recipe is what the system owns; the count of consumers is a fact about this week.

How hierarchy works

How information travelshow to tell what matters

The floor is 11px. Small things get quieter, not smaller: caps, tracking, and soft ink do the work below the title line.

Hierarchy is voice → weight → colour → size. A title differs from its body by switching voice (mono over sans) before it differs by size; neighbours stay within one ladder step.

Top to bottom: eyebrow → title → body → meta. Each step down loses one of weight, size, or ink, never two at once.

A label belongs to what it labels. Air above a heading is at least twice the gap below it, so sections read as blocks, not stripes.

Groups breathe more than members. The space between clusters is at least twice the space inside one: whitespace is the grouping tool, boxes are the fallback.

One left rail per surface. Everything in a card starts on the same 14px inset; in a modal, 20px. Indentation is reserved for quoted or nested content.

Why it looks like that

A heading is not one of its rows. Set it smaller and no heavier than the things underneath and it reads as the weakest item on the panel instead of the break between two groups. It was, and now it isn't.

Weight does the ranking, the hairline does the break. Micro caps at weight 700, the one place micro is bold. Ink stays soft, so the heading outranks its rows without out-shouting them: hierarchy spends voice, then weight, and colour comes later.

The label is underlined, the full width. Not a rule beside the words: that is decoration, and its length is an accident of how long the word happens to be. Under the words it is a rule, and it draws the same line every time. It is the same shape this page uses for its own headings, one scale down, so a rail, a panel and a document all break the same way.

It binds downward. Space above is larger than the space below, so the heading belongs to the group it opens rather than floating between two.

One mark, one aside, no third thing. A colour dot or kind glyph may lead; a count or a switch may ride the far end past the rule. A group heading never takes focus and never wraps.

Two ratiosthey pick the rung, so you don't have to

Proximity. A label sits at least twice as close to the thing it labels as to whatever came before it. That is the whole reason a heading has 24 above and 8 below.

Grouping. Space between groups is at least twice the space inside one. Whitespace does the grouping; a box or a rule is what you reach for only when space alone can't carry it.

Whitespace is a subtraction tool. When a surface feels crowded, remove something. Never compress the gaps to fit more in. A surface keeps its full padding even when it is nearly empty, because that is what makes what's left read as deliberate.

Padding

a ladder outside, optical inside

Space inside a control doesn't come off the ladder. Inner padding is whatever centres the text in the height the ladder already gave you, and that lands on numbers a spacing scale would never produce. Here are all thirteen recipes, exactly as this page renders them. Use one. Don't invent a fourteenth, and don't "tidy" a 7 into an 8.

Three of the thirteen cover most of what you will build. A control on a row takes 2 12 0. Anything that frames content takes 7 14 at the head and 12 14 14 in the body, which is what gives a card its one left rail. A surface you fill takes 20 in a modal and 16 in a side panel, because a panel lives beside your work and a modal has stopped it.

Why the tops and bottoms don't match

A row like 2 12 0 looks like a mistake and isn't. The control's height is fixed by the ladder and the label is centred by flex, so vertical padding is not what centres it. The single pixel on top is the optical drop that caps need to sit on the middle of a line rather than a hair above it. Take it away and every engraved verb in the kit rides high.

Why the sides aren't equal either

Every engraved label carries letter-spacing, and letter-spacing is added after the last character, so a capsule set to 12 and 12 measures 12 on the left and about 12.4 on the right. Buttons, chips and tooltips subtract that trailing space from the right pad and give it back on the left. The table gives the nominal number. The kit's own CSS carries the correction, which is the plainest argument there is for taking the primitive instead of rebuilding it.

When motion is off

less motion, not less product

Ask your system for reduced motion and Eido answers everywhere at once, not surface by surface. Transforms become instant. Fades hold at 80ms, because a fade is how you notice something arrived, and removing it makes overlays teleport. The camera stops flying and simply arrives. The three pulsing dots stop moving and hold at full strength, so the signal stays and only the animation goes.

What deliberately doesn't change: timeline playback still runs at the speed you chose, because that's content you asked to watch rather than an effect. Nothing is ever cut to zero either, only to one millisecond, so everything waiting on an animation still gets told it finished.

How a drag behaves

the same four rules everywhere
When it starts

Cards wait 5. Instruments don't wait at all. A card has to tolerate the wobble in a click, so nothing moves until the pointer has genuinely travelled. You've already aimed at an instrument, so it answers on contact and jumps to where you pressed. Click distance matches the drag threshold exactly, so a shaky click still counts as a click.

drag me

Press and move. Nothing happens for the first 5 pixels, then it commits.

While it runs

The pointer is captured, so the gesture keeps working when your hand leaves the control, leaves the card, or leaves the window. Moves are collapsed to one paint per frame, so a fast drag costs the same as a slow one: a mouse can report far more moves per second than a screen can show, and answering every one of them buys nothing you can see. Nothing is written to your board yet.

You get this for free, and it is worth knowing why: the browser already delivers one pointermove per frame with the samples in between folded into it. Reaching for a timer or a frame gate on top of that is work that buys nothing. Every drag on this page runs through one shared helper that does exactly this and no more.

When you let go

The release point is the answer. The final position is recomputed from where you let go, not from the last frame that happened to be drawn, so nothing lands a pixel behind your hand. Then one change is written, and one undo takes the whole gesture back. Not one per pixel.

When it stops without you

Switch apps, switch tabs, unplug the mouse, or let the system steal the pointer: the gesture ends and the value goes back to where it started. Nothing is left half-dragged, and nothing commits a value you didn't choose.

Why it looks like that

A surface, not a stripe. The old shape was a 2px rule down the left edge, the most borrowed device in software, and it says nothing about this app. A callout is a tinted well one step off the page instead: depth does the separating, which is the argument the whole system already makes about shadows.

Label first, words under it. A label in a left column is fine at three words and terrible at eight: the column has to be as wide as its longest label, and every shorter one leaves a hole. Stacked, it reads the same at any length.

The tint is mixed into what it lies on. 12% of the functional colour over the surface underneath, so a callout on a card and a callout on the board are the same component and still look native to both.

Four tones and no fifth. Attention by default, go for a signpost, avoid for a caution, convo while something is running. Identity is a user's colour and never a callout's.

It never takes focus. A callout tells you something. If it needs a button, it is a modal; if it disappears on its own, it is a toast.

Mods

one card, one instrument

A Mod is a card whose whole body is a control surface. It doesn't hold text and it doesn't read anything upstream. It sits beside your work on a dotted line and changes how the thing it points at behaves. There are eleven of them, and every one is the same card wearing a different instrument.

Every Mod below is drawn at its own floor: 260 wide, and the height its face actually needs. It may grow from here; it may never go under. The eleven numbers are in Sizing · Floors.

Anatomy

head, face, foot
Top to bottom
  • Head, 36 high. The kind dot on the left, which is also the colour control. It picks the accent and how heavily the card wears it, from a bare dot to a fully filled card. The name beside it, editable in place. Settings on the right, hidden until you hover or select. A plus joins it only when the kind can grow and isn't already at its ceiling, so the key is never there to be pressed and refused.
  • Face. One instrument, filling everything between the rules. It is the scroller, so a tall face scrolls inside the card rather than painting over the foot.
  • Foot. One key, the eye. It opens the sentence the Mod is currently telling the model, in plain language, with the live values highlighted.
  • Ports. Left and right, same as every card, though only the right one does anything: a Mod modifies, it never consumes.
The gutter14, on purpose

Head, face and foot all inset 14 from the card edge, so the instrument lines up with the name above it and the eye below it. It's the one number in the card that isn't on the spacing ladder, and it's deliberate. 12 leaves the rail too close to the border; 16 makes a narrow Mod feel starved.

Rows inside the face are 12 apart. Label and value sit on one line, label left, value right. The label yields first and the value keeps 176 to itself, so the number is the last thing to go.

Colour inside a Mod

Everywhere else in Eido, a picked thing wears ink and the twelve identity colours mean nothing functional. Inside a Mod, the accent you chose is that Mod's ink. Steps, stops and the settings key

the part of a Mod you set once
One number, eleven meanings

Steps is how many notches a value has, and it's the one setting that reaches every kind. It's the detents on a slider, the ticks around a dial, the maths behind count's percent and ratio, the stops on a filter and the named levels in a matrix cell. Change it and the whole face rescales. Read a face without knowing its steps and you're reading half of it.

Stops are those notches once they are named. "Never, rarely, often, always" beats "1, 2, 3, 4" every time, so a Mod that can name its stops should.

Behind the settings key

The settings key opens the parts of a Mod you set once and then forget. The step count, the names for each stop, and per part a label and the phrase it contributes to the sentence. Nothing here is a live value. Everything here changes what the live values mean.

Labels are also editable in place, straight on the instrument. The settings key is where you go to rename many at once; a double press on the label is how you fix one.

The eyeand why it's a field, not a read-out

The eye in the foot shows the sentence this Mod is currently telling the model, live values and all. It is also where you can overrule it. If the sentence the system writes isn't the sentence you want, write your own and it's used instead. This is the honest version of "you can see what the AI is being told": you can see it, and you can change it.

The line out of it

A Mod's wire is dotted, wears the Mod purple, and carries a ~ in its midpoint pill. Dotted because a Mod modifies rather than feeds: nothing travels along it, it changes the character of what is already there. One weight for a direct connection, a thinner one for a light touch. The pill is the only place the relationship is written down, and pressing it opens the popover that lets you change it. Full grammar in connections.

Where the line lands decides how far the Mod reaches. On a Conversation it shapes the whole exchange. On one participant in that Conversation it stays private to that voice.

Connections

the line carries the meaning

A line says two things at once, and neither is decoration. Thick or thin is how strong. Colour is what kind: green helps, warm red avoids, purple modifies. Dotting is not a third axis, it comes with the kind: a line that feeds something is solid, and a line that pushes against or reshapes carries nothing along itself, so it is dotted. How a line is drawn

the curve is not freehand
It leaves square

Every line is a cubic whose two control points push straight out of the connectors they start from. It is easy to hit

Spelling and numbers

British throughout. Colour, behaviour, organise, grey. A CSS property name is not user-facing copy, so the token sheet keeps its American spellings and nothing else does.

Numbers speak in mono tabular. Counts, versions, times, coordinates, percentages. Tabular so a changing number never shifts what sits beside it.

In flight is the bare word. Running, Writing, Saving, each with the shared pulse. The pulse carries aliveness, so the word never carries an ellipsis.

Punctuation follows grammar, not position

A hint that is a sentence takes a full stop. A hint that is a fragment takes none. "Tooltips appear after 600ms of real intent." and "solid only inside a confirm" are both right, and neither may borrow the other's ending.

One string per job. If two places ask for the same thing they ask in the same words, because a second phrasing reads as a second feature.

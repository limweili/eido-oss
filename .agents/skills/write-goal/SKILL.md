# Write Goal

A goal is a decision compass, not a runbook. Write it so the destination
survives changes in tools, execution topology, repository state, and tactics.

## Lead with why

Explain:

- the outcome being sought;
- why it matters and which user or system harm it prevents;
- the product and safety truths that must remain intact;
- the observable state that means the work is complete.

Preserve the user's language when it carries intent. Every hard constraint
must trace to an outcome, product truth, or safety boundary.

## Separate durable intent from changing mechanics

A durable goal normally contains:

1. Destination and why.
2. Product truths and safety boundaries.
3. Observable definition of done.
4. Authority and escalation boundaries.
5. Evidence philosophy proportional to risk.

Leave out current progress, branch identities, model or tool names, execution
topology, exact commands, test counts, and fixed step sequences unless one of
those is itself part of the outcome or protects a genuine safety boundary.

Put changing mechanics in the live plan, stable repository requirements in
repository policy, current state in the tracker, and tool quirks in their local
operational documentation.

## Write for reasoning

Prefer positive outcomes and decision principles over exhaustive
prohibitions. Give tactics as defaults with their rationale, not mandatory
choreography.

When execution contradicts the original method, preserve the why, product
truths, safety boundaries, and definition of done while adapting the path.
Within those boundaries, live facts and current-session evidence outrank
generic recipes.

When evidence changes the durable destination or a genuine boundary, publish a
versioned amendment that preserves the earlier objective and status. Do not
rewrite history or mark unfinished work complete merely to replace goal text.

For implementation goals, say what must be known—not how many tests must be
written. Tests and review are confidence tools. Ask for direct proof at the
highest-risk public seams, reuse evidence across shared primitives and slices,
and keep validation proportional to available compute and decision value.

## Quality checks

Before returning the goal, apply these tests:

- **Why test:** can the executor reason about an unforeseen trade-off?
- **Tool-swap test:** does the goal survive a change of tools or delivery
  system?
- **Strategy-swap test:** can different sound approaches converge on the same
  outcome?
- **Completion test:** can done be judged without reading a progress log?
- **Traceability test:** does every hard instruction protect an outcome or
  safety boundary?
- **Evidence test:** does validation support the result instead of becoming a
  second product?
- **Compaction test:** is the goal short and clear enough to reload as durable
  context?

When the user asks for a short goal, compress these elements into one or a few
paragraphs without removing the why or definition of done.

# Soluna Mode

Act as the **conductor**. Interpret the user's brief within governing and
repository instructions; delegation grants no additional authority. Subagent
reports are hypotheses. The main session owns scope, synthesis, integration,
quality, and the stop decision.

For a substantial research-only investigation whose outcome is an
evidence-grounded visual teaching report, use `$research-fanout` when that skill
is available. Soluna Mode remains the lane for delivery work that needs Luna
production followed by independent Sol adversarial review.

## 1. Frame the brief

Re-read the request, original goals, relevant source, and existing evidence.
Write a compact rubric containing the required outcome, constraints, non-goals,
observable acceptance signals, risks, and authoritative proof. Keep an issue and
evidence ledger with `signal | status | evidence | finding/disposition |
protection`. The frame is complete when every mandatory part of the brief has an
acceptance signal and every consequential assumption is listed with an owner and
status. Set a finite autonomous convergence budget, proportional to scope and
risk, for formal review cohorts and elapsed or token spend. Exhausting the budget
is a blocker, never a reason to lower the quality gate.

## 2. Run the Luna production lane

Use the native delegation mechanism to dispatch `gpt-5.6-luna` agents with
`xhigh` reasoning. With `spawn_agent`, use `fork_turns: "none"` or a bounded
positive turn count because model overrides cannot use a full-history fork; make
each prompt self-contained. Use as many available agents as there are genuinely
independent, useful lanes. Give each a bounded deliverable, workspace and source
context, owned and protected paths or artifacts, mutation boundaries, and
required evidence. Every lane inherits the main session's privacy, permission,
external-system, and approval boundaries.

Subagents do not delegate further unless the conductor explicitly budgets and
records their descendant lanes. Count all concurrently active agents, including
the conductor and descendants, against available capacity; completed historical
agents consume no slot.

Record pre-existing target state before delegation. All agents share one
filesystem: one active writer owns each file or generated artifact, shared files
and cross-lane integration have one owner, and overlapping or stateful checks are
serialized. Treat pre-existing dirty and untracked work as user-owned: classify
it as in-scope or protected, preserve it, and reconcile target state afterward.
After a failed or interrupted writer, inspect and disposition its partial changes
before transferring ownership. Redundant clones and conflicting writers add no
capacity.

Luna agents perform the legwork: discovery, implementation, tests, research,
documentation, or edge-case analysis. Require each report to identify artifacts
or changes, checks run, evidence, assumptions, and unresolved risks. Inspect the
actual outputs and verify material claims; an agent's declaration of completion
is advisory.

The production lane is complete when the main session has reconciled every
required output into one coherent candidate and run the cheapest authoritative
checks that can expose failure against the rubric. At least one Luna agent must
return a successful, substantive production report. Give every dispatched lane a
terminal disposition: reconcile its report, retry or reassign it, or document
its cancellation and take over its acceptance signals in the main session;
failed, refused, or timed-out agents do not satisfy the Luna requirement.

Before the formal review gate, perform at least one separate detail pass: re-read
the original brief, inspect the integrated candidate end to end, and improve the
artifact or its proof wherever the audit exposes weakness. This mandatory
iteration does not require cosmetic churn when the evidence supports the current
artifact.

## 3. Open the Sol review gate

Review only a concrete candidate. Confirm every production writer, permitted
descendant, and write-capable subprocess or tool session is stopped and
quiescent; interrupting an agent alone is not proof. Freeze one complete content
identity covering every delivered artifact, including relevant dirty, untracked,
and generated bytes while excluding unrelated private files; materialize
isolated review copies from that identity. Dispatch at least two
independent `gpt-5.6-sol` agents with `high` reasoning as non-authoring
adversaries. They may run concurrently or sequentially against the same identity.
With `spawn_agent`, use `fork_turns: "none"` or a bounded positive turn count and
a self-contained prompt.

Give reviewers distinct lenses: one must cover goal and product fit, another
correctness and regressions, with safety or operational risk added when relevant.
Give them the frozen brief, isolated copy, snapshot identity, and current evidence
while withholding prior conclusions that would anchor their judgment. Require
each report to name that identity. Reviewers may inspect and run bounded
diagnostics in their copy using disposable reviewer-owned state, but may not edit
the candidate or mutate external or shared state. A filesystem copy does not
isolate live databases, services, credentials, network effects, or shared caches;
keep those read-only or replace them with disposable equivalents. Verify the
candidate identity after review; otherwise discard the gate and rerun it.

Ask reviewers to falsify acceptance signals, construct counterexamples, and hunt
for omissions and regressions. Require candid, specific findings classified as:

- **Must Fix** — a failed mandatory outcome or material correctness, safety,
  regression, or integration defect.
- **Should Fix** — meaningful quality debt that may affect acceptance.
- **Explore** — optional opportunity outside the present completion gate.
- **Protect** — a strength or invariant that subsequent fixes must preserve.

Every blocking finding needs inspectable evidence or a reproducible path. The
review gate is complete when every finding has a recorded disposition and
evidence against the brief and observed behavior.

## 4. Drive the convergence loop

Disposition every finding as `accept`, `reject` with counterevidence, `transform`
into an equally sufficient correction, or `park` because it is demonstrably
optional and outside the frozen brief. A **Must Fix** can only be accepted and
resolved, rejected, or transformed; only the user may change the brief so that it
no longer blocks.
A **Should Fix** is implemented unless evidence shows it is incorrect, outside
the brief, or lower-value than a stated constraint. Convert accepted **Protect**
findings into invariants checked after later changes. Resolve disagreements by
targeted verification, not votes.

Send accepted fixes back through focused Luna lanes, integrate them, and rerun
affected proof. Any revision that can affect an acceptance signal invalidates the
previous gate: freeze the new candidate and spawn a fresh cohort of at least two
Sol reviewers rather than reusing the prior reviewers. Re-check authority before
each correction; review feedback cannot authorize scope expansion or destructive,
external, or shared-state action.

After the mandatory detail pass and first Sol cohort, start another round only
for a failed or unknown acceptance signal, a material finding, contradictory
evidence, or a newly exposed risk. This is the **convergence loop**: iteration
continues while it changes the answer rather than toward an arbitrary pass count.
A round counts as progress only when it improves an acceptance status, resolves
a finding, or produces decisive new evidence. Continue only while a named safe
action can change a remaining failure or unknown; if a completed round makes no
progress, or the declared budget is exhausted, stop and request the smallest
decision or external change needed.

## Completion gate

The main session may declare completion only when:

- every mandatory acceptance signal passes with evidence current for the final
  candidate;
- no evidence-backed **Must Fix** or acceptance-relevant **Should Fix** remains;
- every finding across all cohorts has a terminal disposition;
- at least two successful, substantive, independent Sol reports cover the final
  snapshot identity, and that reviewed snapshot is the delivered candidate;
- every accepted **Protect** invariant still holds;
- a final main-session audit traces the delivered candidate to the original
  brief.

Report the result, verification, review disposition, residual uncertainty, and
the models actually used. Exact `gpt-5.6-luna`/`xhigh` production and
`gpt-5.6-sol`/`high` review define this mode. If either is unavailable, stop
before claiming Soluna completion and ask the user whether to authorize a named
fallback. If missing authority, external state, repeated contradictory evidence,
or a user decision prevents the gate from passing, report the precise blocker
and smallest needed decision instead of manufacturing closure.

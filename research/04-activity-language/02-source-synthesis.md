# Source Synthesis

## Inputs Reviewed

This synthesis uses:

- the initial design-system brief,
- the throwaway "Synaptic Flow Grid" direction,
- the attached Rhythm MHTML proposal,
- the user's feedback on that proposal,
- five visual references,
- Eido's current context and canvas foundation docs.

The current proposal code was not reused. Only its product ideas were extracted.

## What The Rhythm Proposal Got Right

The attached Rhythm proposal had several ideas worth preserving:

- **Zoom disclosure.** The artifact can reveal more detail as the user moves from year to month to day to the board itself.
- **Anti-guilt philosophy.** Quiet days should not become failures. The language should celebrate what happened, not shame what did not.
- **Plain verbs.** Distilling activity into a small set of meaningful actions is promising.
- **Board as seed.** The board's own layout and history should drive the output.
- **Locked ink.** Marks should remain stable after they are formed.
- **Show on board.** A day or mark should be able to light up the corresponding cards on the canvas.
- **Playback relationship.** The still artifact and timeline playback can be two views over the same history.

## What Needs To Change

The feedback points away from a polished activity tracker and toward a bolder board portrait system:

- **The calendar grid is too external.** It can make the page legible, but it does not explain the user's board.
- **The visual language was too timid.** The direction needs stronger color, shape, texture, and graphic confidence.
- **Apple Health rings are too literal.** Rings imply daily completion and habit closure. Eido should feel more like a journal.
- **Three verbs are not enough yet.** Capture and connect feel important. Return may or may not be the right third verb.
- **Color cannot be only a verb legend.** The user's board colors and region identities need to flow into the activity artifact.
- **Location is essential.** The artifact should retain where thoughts lived on the board and how regions related.
- **The output should be recognizable without labels.** The user should be able to point to a mark and identify the board region or thought area from its shape and color.

## Visual Reference Translation

### Shape Studies Poster

Source: [assets/shape-studies-poster.png](assets/shape-studies-poster.png)

Useful qualities:

- bold geometric primitives,
- overprinted primary colors,
- rounded rectangles, capsules, circles, and soft bridges,
- visible grain and imperfect registration,
- thin annotation marks that make the page feel studied rather than ornamental.

Eido translation:

- use modular primitives for thought events,
- let overlap create secondary colors,
- add sparse annotation only where it clarifies a research or share view,
- keep the page tactile, not slick.

### Activity Cell Field

Source: [assets/activity-cell-field.png](assets/activity-cell-field.png)

Useful qualities:

- many related glyphs with high individual variation,
- radial fields and dark cores,
- small cells that feel generated from one grammar but not duplicated.

Eido translation:

- activity glyphs can share rules while remaining distinct,
- intensity can appear through density, glow, grain, and core contrast,
- repeated cells should read as a taxonomy of days or sessions.

### Modular Color Studies

Eido translation:

- a period view can become a sheet of board portraits,
- each portrait can compress clusters and regions into modular shapes,

Useful qualities:

- colored clusters and group frames,

## Synthesis

The next direction should be called a **board portrait** before it is called an activity tracker.

The board portrait is a generated visual artifact that compresses a time window of the board into a recognizable graphic study. It can be arranged into periodic views, but its composition is anchored by the board's spatial memory.

It should answer:

- Where on the board did thinking happen?
- Which regions grew, connected, or reactivated?
- What colors and shapes did the user come to recognize?
- Which parts of the board became dense, isolated, or bridged?
- What did the board feel like during that period?

The output should be beautiful because the user shaped the board, not because a generic visual system decorated their data.

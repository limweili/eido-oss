Here's some inspiration of the design language, style & a procedurally generated idea of this.

Really important to note that these are just inspirations, not how I think it should work, but I think there are elements to the following that can heavily influence your Your creation.

“Playful Modular Modernism”

Key traits:
Modular geometric vocabulary: circles, rounded rectangles, capsules, arches, blobs, grids, and tile-like compositions.
Bright CMYK-ish palette: saturated yellow, cyan/blue, red-orange, magenta, green, purple, black, and soft cream/white backgrounds.
Layered transparency: shapes overlap with multiply-like blending, creating secondary colors and a sense of depth.
Soft industrial geometry: lots of rounded corners and pill forms, but arranged with rigorous grid logic.
Analog print texture: halftone grain, fuzzy spray edges, imperfect registration, and screen-print/Risograph noise.
Study-sheet presentation: compositions appear as experiments, thumbnails, grids, swatches, or visual taxonomies rather than single finished illustrations.
Controlled chaos: the layouts are busy and colorful, but still organized by repetition, alignment, and spacing.
Graphic notation accents: scribbles, thin outline boxes, starburst marks, and small typographic labels give it a sketchbook/process feel.
Neo-Bauhaus / postmodern playground mood: abstract, optimistic, tactile, curious, slightly academic but not sterile.

Inputs
The core inputs would be design tokens plus generative parameters.

The important part is that the output should not just be a flat image. It should also return the recipe: seed, palette, shape list, positions, blend modes, texture settings, and layer structure. That lets you regenerate, edit, animate, theme, or turn the artwork into UI assets.
Core Generator Model
I’d make the engine work like this:
seed
-> choose palette
-> choose grid
-> choose composition archetype
-> place large anchor shapes
-> add medium connective shapes
-> add small dots/capsules/details
-> apply overlap/blending
-> add texture/noise/misregistration
-> optionally add labels/annotations
-> export SVG/Canvas/PNG + metadata
The design language becomes strongest when both systems share the same primitives:
Color + Grid + Shape + Layering + Texture + Annotation
That’s the spine of the style. The design system makes it usable; the procedural engine makes it alive.

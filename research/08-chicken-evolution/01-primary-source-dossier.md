# Chicken evolution: a primary-source evidence dossier

**Status:** point-in-time research synthesis, 2 August 2026

**Scope:** the defensible evolutionary backbone from deep animal ancestry to the domestic chicken, the living *Gallus* radiation, domestication and introgression, global dispersal, uncertainties, and a card set designed for an Eido research board.

**Source rule:** inferential claims below are tied to original research papers. NCBI Taxonomy and AviList are used only as current nomenclatural scaffolds, not as evidence that a phylogenetic hypothesis is true.

## Executive synthesis

1. A chicken is a living theropod dinosaur. Its defensible backbone is `Archosauria → Dinosauria → Saurischia → Theropoda → Coelurosauria → Maniraptora → Paraves → Avialae → crown birds → Neognathae → Galloanserae → Galliformes → Phasianidae → Gallus → domestic chicken`. Named fossils placed near this route are side relatives or minimum-age evidence, not demonstrated direct ancestors. Morphological analyses find no single “bird moment”: bird-like features accumulated piecemeal through theropod evolution, alongside sustained body-size reduction before early birds ([Brusatte et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.08.034); [Lee et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1252243)).
2. Crown birds had already begun diversifying by the end of the Cretaceous. The 66.8–66.7 Ma fossil *Asteriornis* lies close to the last common ancestor of living landfowl and waterfowl, proving that the galloanseran total lineage was present immediately before the K–Pg extinction ([Field et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2096-0)). Genome-scale bird trees strongly support `Palaeognathae + (Galloanserae + Neoaves)`, while the rapid early radiation leaves localized gene-tree conflict ([Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451); [Prum et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15697); [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1)).
3. The galliform family backbone is unusually secure: `Megapodiidae + (Cracidae + (Numididae + (Odontophoridae + Phasianidae)))`. A 3,574-UCE analysis of 130 taxa recovered these family splits with 100% bootstrap support, but its dates—crown Galliformes about 71.7 Ma and crown Phasianidae about 35.4 Ma—remain model- and fossil-calibration-dependent ([Chen et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-021-01935-1)).
4. Four wild *Gallus* species live today: green (*G. varius*), red (*G. gallus*), grey (*G. sonneratii*), and Sri Lanka/Ceylon junglefowl (*G. lafayettii*). Grey + Sri Lanka is the stable sister pair. Whether green or red junglefowl split first is unresolved because incomplete lineage sorting and ancient gene flow make different genomic partitions support different trees ([Lawal et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-020-0738-1); [Tiley et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40657-020-00194-w); [Mariadassou et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2020.107044)).
5. Modern domestic chickens derive primarily from one surviving *G. g. spadiceus*-related red-junglefowl lineage, associated today with southwestern China, northern Thailand, and Myanmar. This does **not** prove an exact domestication site, a start date, or that humans made only one domestication attempt. The reported 9,500 ± 3,300-year genomic split is a population-divergence estimate and an upper bound, not an archaeological domestication date ([Wang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-020-0349-y)).
6. Domestication history is a network, not a terminal twig on a clean tree. After the principal founding event, regional chickens admixed with several red-junglefowl subspecies and occasionally with grey, Sri Lanka, and green junglefowl. The grey-junglefowl-derived **BCO2** haplotype responsible for yellow skin is the clearest locus-level example ([Eriksson et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1000010); [Lawal et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-020-0738-1)).
7. Archaeology is younger and firmer than many genetic headlines. A conservative reassessment of more than 600 sites placed the earliest unambiguous domestic-chicken context at Ban Non Wat, Thailand, about 1650–1250 BCE. Grain cultivation may have created a commensal pathway, but correlation is not direct proof of mechanism ([Peters et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2121978119)). Earlier northern-China claims were undermined by morphology and authenticated DNA showing that key remains were pheasants ([Eda et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2016.01.012); [Barton et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-59316-5)).

## How to read the evidence

### Confidence labels

| Label | Meaning here |
| --- | --- |
| **High** | Recovered by multiple independent, genome-scale or broad cladistic datasets; no live alternative changes the central claim. |
| **Moderate** | Strong evidence from one broad study, or agreement whose numerical date/placement is model-sensitive. |
| **Contested** | Credible primary studies recover incompatible results, or authentication and sampling materially change the answer. |

### Date labels

- **Observed minimum:** a directly dated fossil or archaeological specimen. It proves the lineage or object existed by then; it is not the origin date.
- **Inferred split:** a molecular-clock or demographic estimate. It depends on calibration, mutation rate, generation time, model, and sampled populations.
- **First secure archaeological appearance:** the oldest presently defensible human-associated context in a conservative synthesis. It can move earlier with new evidence.

### Backbone confidence and interpretation

| Segment | Confidence | Evidence and qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Archosauria → bird-line archosaurs → Dinosauria | High for nesting; moderate for dates | *Teleocrater*, about 245 Ma, reveals that several “dinosaur-like” ankle and locomotor features arose early on the bird side after the crocodile split; it is a side relative, not an ancestor ([Nesbitt et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22037)). |
| Dinosauria → Theropoda → coelurosaur/maniraptoran line → Avialae | High for the broad nesting | Large morphological matrices show a long continuum of trait assembly rather than a saltational origin of birds ([Brusatte et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.08.034)). Sustained miniaturization occurred across roughly 50 million years and 12 branches on the theropod route to birds ([Lee et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1252243)). |
| Placement of individual Jurassic paravians | Contested | New fossils repeatedly move the membership and branching order of deinonychosaurs and early avialans. *Archaeopteryx* is a Late Jurassic early avialan-grade taxon, not a demonstrated direct ancestor; even its exact neighborhood is analysis-sensitive ([Rauhut et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.4191); [Pei et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.06.105)). |
| Crown Aves → Palaeognathae + Neognathae; Neognathae → Galloanserae + Neoaves | High for topology; moderate for exact dates | Independent genome-scale datasets agree on the backbone, although short early internodes retain incomplete-lineage-sorting and gene-tree conflict ([Jarvis et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1253451); [Prum et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15697); [Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1)). |
| Galloanserae present before K–Pg | High as a minimum-age statement | *Asteriornis* is 66.8–66.7 Ma and close to the galloanseran last common ancestor. It does not prove that modern Galliformes themselves existed at that date ([Field et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2096-0)). |
| Galliform family backbone | High for topology; moderate for dates | Thousands of UCE loci across every living genus recover the five-family order above; fossil-calibrated absolute ages have broad model sensitivity ([Chen et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-021-01935-1)). |
| Bambusicola + Gallus | Moderate–high | Whole-genome/UCE analyses place bamboo partridges as the sister genus to *Gallus*, with a model-dependent split near 15–16 Ma ([Tiley et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40657-020-00194-w)). |
| Grey + Sri Lanka junglefowl | High | Recovered across competing genome-scale *Gallus* analyses. |
| Whether red or green junglefowl is deepest within living Gallus | Contested | Lawal and Tiley favor `(Green,(Red,(Grey,Sri Lanka)))`; Mariadassou favors `(Red,(Green,(Grey,Sri Lanka)))`. Introgression and incomplete lineage sorting can make a high-bootstrap tree wrong as a species history ([Lawal et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-020-0738-1); [Tiley et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40657-020-00194-w); [Mariadassou et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2020.107044)). |
| Domestic chicken nested in red junglefowl | High | The strongest whole-genome sampling supports a primary *spadiceus*-related source, followed by regional and cross-species gene flow ([Wang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-020-0349-y)). |

### The reticulate overlay

The species tree must be paired with these gene-flow edges:

```text
G. g. spadiceus-related population ═════▶ principal surviving domestic lineage

G. g. murghi     ─ ─ ─▶ South Asian chickens; also strong recent breed input
G. g. bankiva    ─ ─ ─▶ Indonesian chickens
G. g. gallus     ─ ─ ─▶ Indonesian chickens
G. g. jabouillei ─ ─ ─▶ some Chinese chickens

G. sonneratii    · · ·▶ multiple domestic tracts, including BCO2/yellow skin
G. lafayettii    · · ·▶ fewer, geographically restricted candidate tracts
G. varius        · · ·▶ rare compelling candidate tract(s)

domestic chicken · · ·▶ modern nominally wild red-junglefowl populations
```

Wang et al. estimated geographically structured secondary red-junglefowl ancestry—about 3.8–22.4% *murghi*-related ancestry in sampled South Asian chickens, 1.6–6.5% *bankiva* and 4.8–10.7% *gallus* in sampled Indonesian chickens, and 1.3–6.2% *jabouillei* in sampled Chinese chickens. These are population/model estimates, not universal species fractions ([Wang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-020-0349-y)).

Lawal et al. combined local topology weighting, allele-sharing statistics, divergence, differentiation, haplotypes, and tract lengths to identify grey-to-domestic, rarer Sri Lanka-to-domestic, and one compelling green-to-domestic case. Genome-wide D-statistic values should **not** be relabeled as clean ancestry percentages: closely related donors, incomplete lineage sorting, and bidirectional gene flow confound that interpretation ([Lawal et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-020-0738-1)).

## Domestication: what the evidence supports

### Principal source, not a simplistic “single origin”

Wang et al.'s 863 genomes included all four living wild *Gallus* species, all five sampled red-junglefowl subspecies, and broad domestic diversity. Multiple analyses converged on a domestic clade closest to *G. g. spadiceus*. The strongest safe statement is:

> Modern chickens derive primarily from one surviving *G. g. spadiceus*-related founding lineage, after which local red-junglefowl populations and other *Gallus* species contributed additional ancestry.

“All chickens were domesticated once at location X in year Y” exceeds the data. Modern genomes cannot recover an extinct source population, locate the first management settlement, distinguish every failed domestication attempt, or make a demographic split equal a cultural process ([Wang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-020-0349-y)).

### Archaeology and the grain-commensal hypothesis

Peters et al. reassessed roughly 600 sites across 89 countries using identification quality, osteometry, stratigraphy, context, iconography, and texts. Their conservative chronology linked the first secure domestic contexts in mainland Southeast Asia with dry-rice and millet cultivation. Grain plots, stores, and fallows plausibly attracted red junglefowl and increased recurring human contact. That is a coherent commensal hypothesis, not an experiment demonstrating cause ([Peters et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2121978119)).

The northern-China case shows why authentication matters. Xiang et al. reported short ancient mitochondrial sequences and proposed chickens near 10,000 BP ([Xiang et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1411882111)). Morphological reassessment found candidate chicken bones rare and absent from the earliest phases ([Eda et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2016.01.012)); authenticated DNA later identified purported Dadiwan “chickens” as ring-necked pheasants ([Barton et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-59316-5)). The old claim belongs on a **debate** branch, not on the main chronology.

### Modern “wild” reference birds are not pristine

Historical red junglefowl collected between 1874 and 1939 retain less domestic ancestry than many modern nominally wild populations. This makes present-day reference panels imperfect proxies for pre-domestication populations and can inflate certainty or mislocalize sources. The robust conclusion is temporal increase in domestic introgression; extreme individual ancestry percentages remain sensitive to reference and local sampling ([Wu et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1010551)).

## Evidence types: what each can and cannot answer

| Evidence | Strongest use | Main failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparative fossil anatomy | Places extinct side relatives and supplies observed minimum ages. | Sparse preservation; character convergence; a close relative is not a direct ancestor. |
| Stratigraphy and direct radiometric dating | Establishes when a specimen existed. | Context may be reworked; indirect layer dates can misdate a bone. |
| Genome-scale species trees | Resolves broad nesting and exposes gene-tree discordance. | Concatenation can hide incomplete lineage sorting; introgression turns a tree into an approximation. |
| Molecular clocks / demographic models | Estimates otherwise invisible divergence windows. | Results depend on calibration, mutation rate, generation time, structure, and model; splits are not domestication events. |
| Ancient DNA | Tests taxonomic identity, continuity, and historical ancestry directly. | Contamination, short fragments, small samples, and postmortem damage; replication/authentication are essential. |
| Morphology / osteometry / ZooMS | Distinguishes archaeological bird remains and broadens sample coverage. | Closely related galliform bones overlap; reference coverage and preservation matter. |
| Archaeological context | Shows human association, treatment, diet, ritual, and movement. | “Found at a settlement” does not by itself prove domestication; intrusive bones can be much younger than the layer. |
| Modern population genomics | Maps surviving ancestry and gene flow at high resolution. | Extinct sources are invisible; modern wild populations can be feral-introgressed; sample geography biases localization. |

## Eido-ready card set

Each entry is deliberately one claim. Parenthetical text gives confidence and the strongest link. Suggested edges let the board preserve argument structure rather than become a pile of notes.

### A. Deep evolutionary backbone

- **TREE-01 — Chicken is a living theropod dinosaur.** Broad morphological phylogenies nest birds within coelurosaurian maniraptoran theropods. Bird anatomy accumulated piecemeal; there was no single anatomical leap. (**High**; [Brusatte 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.08.034)). Link → TREE-02, TREE-03.
- **TREE-02 — Miniaturization preceded early birds.** The lineage toward birds shows sustained body-size reduction for about 50 Myr across roughly 12 branches. (**High trend, model-dependent rate**; [Lee 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1252243)). Link → TREE-03.
- **TREE-03 — Fossils illuminate nodes; they are not known ancestors.** *Teleocrater*, *Archaeopteryx*, and *Asteriornis* should sit on side branches with dates and roles. (**High methodological rule**; [Nesbitt 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22037), [Field 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2096-0)).
- **TREE-04 — Crown-bird backbone.** `Palaeognathae + (Galloanserae + Neoaves)` is strongly recovered; early short internodes retain genomic conflict. (**High topology**; [Stiller 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1)). Link → TREE-05.
- **TREE-05 — Galloanserae crossed the K–Pg boundary.** *Asteriornis* at 66.8–66.7 Ma is close to the landfowl–waterfowl ancestor. (**High observed minimum**; [Field 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2096-0)). Link → TREE-06.
- **TREE-06 — Galliform family backbone.** `Megapodiidae + (Cracidae + (Numididae + (Odontophoridae + Phasianidae)))`. (**High topology, moderate dates**; [Chen 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-021-01935-1)). Link → TREE-07.
- **TREE-07 — Gallus's nearest sampled genus is Bambusicola.** Genome/UCE work supports a ~15–16 Ma model-dependent split. (**Moderate–high**; [Tiley 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40657-020-00194-w)). Link → TREE-08.
- **TREE-08 — Four living wild Gallus species.** Red, green, grey, and Sri Lanka junglefowl; domestic chicken is nested in red-junglefowl diversity. (**High**; [Wang 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-020-0349-y)). Link → DEBATE-01, DOM-01.

### B. Domestication and gene flow

- **DOM-01 — Principal surviving source.** Modern domestic chickens derive primarily from a *G. g. spadiceus*-related population. (**High for sampled surviving lineages**; [Wang 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-020-0349-y)). Link → DOM-02, NET-01.
- **DOM-02 — A genomic split is not domestication.** `9,500 ± 3,300 years` estimates population divergence, not when or where people began managing chickens. (**High interpretive constraint**; [Wang 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-020-0349-y)). Link ↔ ARCH-01.
- **ARCH-01 — Earliest secure domestic context.** Ban Non Wat, Thailand, ~1650–1250 BCE, in the current conservative global synthesis. (**Moderate–high**; [Peters 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2121978119)). Link → ARCH-02, DISP-01.
- **ARCH-02 — Grain-commensal pathway is plausible, not proven.** Cultivation may have concentrated food and repeated junglefowl–human contact. (**Moderate hypothesis**; [Peters 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2121978119)).
- **NET-01 — Domestic history is a network.** Local red-junglefowl subspecies contributed regional ancestry after the principal founding lineage spread. (**High**; [Wang 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-020-0349-y)). Link → NET-02.
- **NET-02 — Other Gallus species also contributed loci.** Grey-to-domestic tracts are best supported; Sri Lanka and green cases are rarer. (**Moderate–high locus-level**; [Lawal 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-020-0738-1)).
- **NET-03 — Yellow skin is a hybrid legacy.** A grey-junglefowl-derived regulatory **BCO2** haplotype causes yellow skin in many domestic chickens. (**High locus-level**; [Eriksson 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1000010)). Link → NET-02.
- **NET-04 — “Wild” is now porous.** Modern red-junglefowl often carry more domestic ancestry than historical museum birds. (**High temporal direction; percentages sample-sensitive**; [Wu 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1010551)). Link → OPEN-02.

### C. Debates and dispersal

- **DEBATE-01 — Red-first or green-first?** Grey + Sri Lanka is stable, but genome-scale studies disagree on whether red or green junglefowl is deepest. Show a fork labelled unresolved. (**Contested**; [Lawal 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-020-0738-1), [Mariadassou 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2020.107044)).
- **DEBATE-02 — Northern China ~10,000 BP claim failed authentication tests.** An initial mtDNA claim was challenged morphologically; authenticated sequences identified key remains as pheasants. (**Contested historical claim; main timeline excludes it**; [Xiang 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1411882111), [Eda 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2016.01.012), [Barton 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-59316-5)). Link → ARCH-01.
- **DISP-01 — Westward spread followed secure Southeast Asian domestication contexts.** Supported appearances proceed through South Asia and Mesopotamia toward the Mediterranean and Africa. (**Moderate**; [Peters 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2121978119)).
- **DISP-02 — Europe's “early chickens” often dated younger.** Direct AMS dating rejected most early claims; secure Mediterranean arrival begins around the eighth/seventh centuries BCE. (**High revision**; [Best 2022](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2021.90)).
- **DISP-03 — Arrival did not equal food animal.** In parts of Europe, special treatment preceded routine consumption by centuries. (**Moderate contextual inference**; [Best 2022](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2021.90)).
- **DEBATE-03 — Pre-Columbian Chile remains unresolved.** A directly dated bone is real; its claimed Polynesian genetic signal is disputed as possible modern contamination. (**Contested**; [Storey 2007](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0703993104), [Thomson 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1320412111)).

### D. Open-question cards

- **OPEN-01 — Where was the actual first management population?** Modern *spadiceus* affinity defines a broad surviving source region, not a settlement. Need directly dated ancient genomes from mainland Southeast Asia before and during 2000 BCE.
- **OPEN-02 — What did pre-domestication red junglefowl look like genomically?** Modern wild populations are contaminated by domestic backcrossing. Need historical and archaeological reference panels across all five subspecies.
- **OPEN-03 — Which Gallus topology survives explicit network models?** Test red-first and green-first trees with fossil-aware dating, ancient samples, and methods jointly modeling incomplete lineage sorting and gene flow.
- **OPEN-04 — Which introgressed tracts changed phenotype?** **BCO2** is secure; most other proposed adaptive functions need genotype-to-phenotype validation.
- **OPEN-05 — Was egg production deliberately selected early?** High Central Asian eggshell abundance suggests regular laying, but seasonality, husbandry, and selection need direct isotopic, medullary-bone, and ancient-genomic evidence.
- **OPEN-06 — Did chickens reach South America before Europeans?** Resolve the Chile specimen with independently replicated libraries, damage patterns, nuclear capture, isotopes, and additional directly dated bones.

## Claims that are not safe to make

- “*Archaeopteryx* evolved into chickens.” It is an early avialan-grade side relative; ancestry is not demonstrated.
- “Chickens stopped being dinosaurs.” Birds are the surviving dinosaur branch.
- “*Asteriornis* was the first chicken.” It is near the galloanseran ancestor and predates the landfowl/waterfowl split or lies close to it, not within *Gallus*.
- “Galliformes began exactly 71.7 million years ago.” That is one fossil-calibrated model estimate with uncertainty.
- “The Gallus species tree is settled.” The red-versus-green basal split is actively disputed.
- “Chicken domestication happened 9,500 years ago.” That number is a demographic population-split estimate.
- “All chickens came from one event at one known place.” The data identify a principal surviving source lineage, while source locality, extinct lineages, human attempts, and later admixture remain unresolved.
- “Domestic chickens are 12% grey junglefowl” (or any single universal percentage). Allele-sharing statistics and regional samples do not justify a species-wide fraction.
- “The oldest archaeological chicken proves the start of domestication.” It is a minimum secure appearance.
- “Northern China domesticated chickens 10,000 years ago.” The key evidence was not authenticated as chicken.
- “Polynesians definitely brought chickens to pre-Columbian Chile,” or the inverse. The specimen date and genetic interpretation currently point in different evidentiary directions.

### Nomenclatural scaffolds

- [NCBI Taxonomy: *Gallus gallus*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Taxonomy/Browser/wwwtax.cgi?command=show&id=9031&mode=node) supplies a reproducible database lineage and treats “chicken” under *G. gallus*. NCBI explicitly does not claim to be an authoritative nomenclatural code.
- [AviList v2025b](https://explore.avilist.org/) supplies the current global bird-list treatment of the four living *Gallus* species and five red-junglefowl subspecies. Domestic nomenclature varies; *Gallus gallus domesticus* is useful communication, not a universally settled rank.

## Board layout recommendation

Use a left-to-right reading path with four aligned bands:

1. **Deep time tree** — one narrow vertical spine from Archosauria to Galloanserae, with fossil cards offset as side branches and date chips.
2. **Galliform / Gallus focus** — expand the five-family landfowl tree, then a deliberately unresolved red/green fork. Put the two candidate topologies side by side rather than choosing one silently.
3. **Domestication network** — a thick *spadiceus*-related arrow into domestic chicken, thinner regional red-junglefowl arrows, dotted other-species introgression arrows, and a backward domestic-to-wild arrow.
4. **Human timeline and map** — align secure archaeological appearances below the genomic network. Put rejected/contested claims in an amber “debate shelf,” never inline on the main timeline.

Visually encode evidence rather than topic alone: solid borders for directly observed fossils/dates, standard borders for multi-method inference, dashed borders for open questions, and amber borders for contested claims. Every numeric date should carry a small `fossil minimum`, `model estimate`, or `archaeological appearance` tag.
